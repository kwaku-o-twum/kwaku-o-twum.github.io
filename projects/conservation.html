Case study 04 / Conservation governance

Understanding conservation on working landscapes

Examining how policy instruments and landowner decisions shape participation.

ContextUniversity of Waterloo and conservation collaborators

The question

What needed to change?

Conservation beyond protected areas depends on how policy arrangements fit the people and landscapes involved. Restrictions, property rights, trust, incentives and the duration of agreements all matter to participation.

My contribution

What I brought to the work.

I co-authored research in Rangeland Ecology & Management and an in-press FACETS paper on term conservation easements. With Andrea Olive, I provide geospatial analysis and lead emerging digital-technology work in conservation research. With the Alberta Conservation Association, I analyse responses from 1,338 landowners and lead manuscript development on hunting access to private land.
